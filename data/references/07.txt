The city library opens at nine every morning, but the first visitors usually arrive a little earlier and wait by the glass doors. Most of them are students carrying heavy bags, and a few are retired teachers who come for the newspapers. Inside, the ground floor is quiet except for the soft clicking of keyboards. Upstairs, the reading room has long wooden tables and tall windows that face the river. On sunny days the light falls across the shelves and makes the old spines glow. The librarian, a tall woman with grey hair, knows almost everyone by name and always asks how their week is going. By noon the room fills up, and finding an empty chair becomes a small competition. Still, nobody raises their voice. People slide books across the table, whisper their thanks, and go back to reading. When the library closes at eight, the lights go off one floor at a time, and the building settles into silence until the next morning.
